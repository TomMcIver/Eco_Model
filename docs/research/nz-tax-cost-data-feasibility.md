# Public data feasibility for aggregate costing of the under-25 exemption

**Reviewed:** 28 October 2026  
**Scope:** Publicly released New Zealand aggregate tables for a static exemption of PAYE salary and wages paid to people under 25. No revenue estimate is produced.

## Finding

**No-go for a defensible national point estimate or informative uncertainty interval from the public tables verified here.** The official material provides useful but separate marginal summaries: IRD wage and salary totals/counts by income band; IRD taxable income and tax distributions; age-specific weekly wage medians from Stats NZ's HLFS income work; and household income statistics. The released descriptions and tables do not establish a recurring public joint distribution of age, annual qualifying PAYE earnings, other taxable income, tax-unit liabilities, and relevant credits. The separate marginals do not identify that joint distribution. Combining them with an independence assumption or fabricated records would yield assumption-driven results, not a data-identified cost.

This conclusion is about what the published sources verified in this review support. The IRD downloadable workbooks are public, but the XLSX binaries could not be retrieved or inspected in this environment: the web fetcher rejected the workbook content type, and a direct read-only download attempt failed at the network receive stage. Therefore, sheet names, exact row/column dimensions, hidden sheets, and any unadvertised cross-tabs remain unverified. A table-by-table review of downloaded workbooks is still a follow-up if the files can be fetched in the project's ingestion environment.

## Why the marginal tables are insufficient

The agreed policy applies to qualifying PAYE salary and wages while the person is under 25. For each affected tax unit, the static income-tax change depends on its annual qualifying earnings, other taxable income, tax-year schedule, and applicable credits/adjustments. In general, a tax schedule is nonlinear, so the total change cannot be computed from a median wage, an overall wage-band distribution, or separate age and taxable-income totals.

The public sources have different units and periods: annual administrative wage bands; annual tax-year taxable-income records; survey medians for a pay period in a June quarter; and household-level income over survey-specific reference periods. There is no verified public key or joint table with which to combine these into the affected under-25 tax units. A simple product of age shares and income-band shares would impose independence between age, qualifying salary/wages, and other income. The reviewed sources provide no empirical basis for that assumption.

Nor do the current marginals, as documented, support informative bounds. Bounds would require compatible counts/totals across the same population and period, plus enough restrictions on the joint allocation of qualifying income and other taxable income to bound the nonlinear tax change. With only separate medians and overall band totals, the allocation across under-25 tax units remains unknown. Any range derived by choosing such an allocation would be a scenario/sensitivity result conditional on that assumption, not a data-only uncertainty interval.

## Recommended next data step

Before aggregate costing, inspect the downloaded IRD workbooks and Stats NZ release files in the project ingestion environment and record exact sheet/table names and dimensions. If they still lack the necessary cross-tab, request a privacy-safe aggregate from IRD/Stats NZ with, at minimum:

- tax year and age (single years 15-24, or the finest safe groups that preserve the policy boundary);
- count of tax units/individuals and total annual qualifying PAYE salary/wages by band, with bands aligned to the policy year's tax thresholds;
- joint bands for other taxable income (or taxable income excluding qualifying wages), so the baseline and policy liability can be recomputed;
- ideally, baseline tax and relevant credits/benefit/loan categories where those effects are included;
- suppression flags, sampling/administrative coverage, definition notes, and revision/vintage metadata.

If only grouped public data can be released, first specify the feasible partial-identification problem and calculate bounds from compatible joint constraints. Use survey replicate weights or published sampling errors where applicable and propagate band-allocation and suppression uncertainty. Do not report a single point estimate unless the within-band and other-income assumptions are explicit and the result is labelled as assumption-dependent. Keep these calculations static: no employment, hours, wages, employer, consumption, GDP, or financing response is identified by these tables.

## Conclusion

The public official releases verified here support component summaries and aggregate controls, but not a defensible aggregate static revenue estimate for the agreed under-25 PAYE exemption. **Do not publish a point estimate or imply a data-derived confidence interval from the available marginals.** Implement the exact individual calculator; report aggregate revenue as not estimable from the currently verified public joint distributions unless workbook inspection or a new aggregate data release establishes adequate coverage.

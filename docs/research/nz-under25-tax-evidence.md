# NZ under-25 employment-income tax exemption: rules and public costing evidence

**Research reviewed:** 28 October 2026  
**Scope:** Static tax mechanics and public aggregate data for a proposed exemption for individuals under 25. This is not a revenue estimate or causal/behavioural analysis.

## Findings

1. New Zealand's individual income tax schedule is progressive and currently applies the same published marginal rates to individuals regardless of age. Inland Revenue lists the rates in force from 1 April 2025 as 10.5% to $15,600, 17.5% to $53,500, 30% to $78,100, 33% to $180,000, and 39% above that. Rates must be selected by income-tax year; a future policy year must use the law then in force, not today's schedule by default. [IRD individual tax rates](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-individuals/tax-codes-and-tax-rates-for-individuals/tax-rates-for-individuals)

2. The Income Tax Act's employment-income definition is broader than ordinary wages: section CE 1 includes salary or wages, allowances, bonuses, extra pay, gratuities, employee share-scheme benefits, directors' fees, loss-of-employment compensation, and other money benefits derived in connection with employment or service. A policy must state exactly which of these qualify. [Income Tax Act 2007, s CE 1 (official legislation PDF)](https://legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2007/0097/latest/096be8ed81f4f65a.pdf)

3. Self-employment is taxed on net profit after eligible business expenses and is reported through an individual return. It is distinct from employment income in Inland Revenue's ordinary guidance, but a policy could explicitly include or exclude it; the simulator must make that a scenario parameter rather than infer it from a person's job title. [IRD self-employed](https://www.ird.govt.nz/roles/self-employed), [IRD employee or self-employed](https://www.ird.govt.nz/roles/employees/self-employed-or-employee)

4. ACC earners' levies are separate from income tax even though PAYE withholding combines them. Inland Revenue lists the earners' levy at 1.75% up to $156,641 liable earnings for 1 April 2026–31 March 2027, with different rates and caps by year. A tax-only exemption should leave ACC unchanged unless the policy explicitly changes levy liability. Self-employed ACC levies have separate liable-income rules and caps. [IRD ACC earners' levy rates](https://www.ird.govt.nz/acclevy), [ACC levy calculation](https://www.acc.co.nz/for-business/received-an-invoice/calculate-your-levies)

5. Student-loan deductions are also separate from income tax: for the 2026 tax year, salary/wage borrowers generally repay 12% above the annual $24,128 threshold (with pay-period withholding rules; secondary income is treated differently). A tax exemption alone does not imply a student-loan exemption. These rules and thresholds need to be versioned by tax year. [IRD student-loan deductions on wages](https://www.ird.govt.nz/repaying-my-student-loan-when-i-earn-salary-or-wages), [IRD self-employed or other student-loan income](https://www.ird.govt.nz/repaying-my-student-loan-when-i-am-self-employed-or-earn-other-income)

6. Transfers and tax credits need a separate calculation layer. Main benefits can be abated against gross income under household- and benefit-specific rules. Working for Families uses family-scheme income, which can exceed taxable income, and eligibility/abatement depends on family composition and payment type. Some benefit payments are taxable and included in family income. Consequently, the simulator must not assume the tax reduction is the whole change in disposable income, nor assume an income-tax exemption automatically changes benefit or WFF means tests. The statutory definition and any amendments enacted for the policy year determine that interaction. [MSD current main-benefit income abatements](https://map.workandincome.govt.nz/map/deskfile/income-abatements-and-exemptions/main-benefits-income-abatement-thresholds/main-benefits-income-abatement-thresholds-current.html), [IRD Working for Families income overview](https://www.ird.govt.nz/working-for-families/all-about-working-for-families/overview), [IRD government payments and WFF](https://www.ird.govt.nz/working-for-families/types/government-payments), [IRD interpretation statement IS 26/12](https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/interpretation-statements/2026/is-26-12)

7. Public data are useful but currently do not establish a sufficiently detailed, verified joint distribution for a defensible national cost of this exact policy. Inland Revenue publishes annual taxable-income counts, taxable income, and tax by income band using administrative tax/PAYE records; its 2026 workbook covers tax years through 2025 and revises 2021–24. It separately publishes wage/salary counts and totals by income band through tax year 2026. Those releases are valuable control totals but their documented tables are not an age-under-25-by-eligible-employment-income cross-tab with all other income and tax-credit interactions. [IRD taxable-income distribution](https://www.ird.govt.nz/about-us/tax-statistics/revenue-refunds/income-distribution), [IRD tax-on-taxable-income datasets](https://www.ird.govt.nz/about-us/tax-statistics/revenue-refunds/income-distribution/tax-on-taxable-income-datasets), [IRD wage/salary distributions](https://www.ird.govt.nz/about-us/tax-statistics/revenue-refunds/wage-salary-distributions), [IRD wage/salary datasets](https://www.ird.govt.nz/about-us/tax-statistics/revenue-refunds/wage-salary-distributions/wage-and-salary-statistics-datasets)

8. Stats NZ provides useful age-specific employment statistics and annual June-quarter income estimates from the Household Labour Force Survey (HLFS), including wages/salary, self-employment, and transfers. Standard labour releases give employment by age; June income releases provide income-source summaries/subpopulation estimates. These are survey estimates with sampling error and imputation, not a public person-level administrative tax file. The HILS (which replaced HES from 2024/25) supports analysis by age and employment status and its public outputs suppress small cells; its weighting uses IDI administrative benchmarks, but that does not make IDI microdata public. [Stats NZ HLFS sources and methods](https://www.stats.govt.nz/assets/Methods/Household-Labour-Force-Survey-sources-and-methods-2025/household-labour-force-survey-sources-and-methods-2025.pdf), [Stats NZ labour market statistics: June 2026](https://www.stats.govt.nz/information-releases/labour-market-statistics-june-2026-quarter/), [Stats NZ labour market statistics income: June 2026](https://www.stats.govt.nz/information-releases/labour-market-statistics-income-june-2026-quarter/), [Stats NZ HILS DataInfo+](https://datainfoplus.stats.govt.nz/item/nz.govt.stats/1ed1e923-4c10-407c-95a0-192be61afcf2)

9. Inland Revenue has published an OIA response with taxable income, calculated tax, and taxpayer counts for age bands 14–15, 16–17, and 18–19, and 20–21 for historical years, but it notes it does not hold the total population denominators for those age groups. This is useful evidence that age-tabulated administrative extracts may be obtainable, but it is not a complete, recurring public series covering ages 22–24 or employment-income-only tax effects. A scoped OIA request for aggregate age × income-source × income-band tables is a possible follow-up; availability and disclosure approval are not guaranteed. [IRD OIA response: income tax by age groups under 21](https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/oia-responses/march-2025/income-tax-collected-from-age-groups-under-21.pdf)

10. Stats NZ's 2024/25 HILS release has a current quality notice: on 29 September 2026 Stats NZ reported errors in some highest-qualification processing with flow-on effects to household-income-based statistics. Do not use affected HILS estimates without checking the notice and corrected release status. [Stats NZ HILS error notification](https://www.stats.govt.nz/reports/error-notification-for-household-income-and-living-survey-hils-data-for-year-ended-june-2025/)

## Policy mechanics to encode

Use a tax-year-specific schedule and model annual liability, rather than treating PAYE withholding as final tax. For an individual with total taxable income `y`, and qualifying exempt income `e` as defined by the scenario, a simple tax-only counterfactual is:

```text
baseline_income_tax = T_y(y)
policy_income_tax   = T_y(y - e)
static_income_tax_change = baseline_income_tax - policy_income_tax
```

`T_y` must implement the income-tax-year's marginal schedule and any applicable annual credits/adjustments. The model must preserve non-qualifying income and losses as required by the enacted rules. This calculation is exact only after the policy defines eligibility age (age on each payday, at tax-year end, or another rule), the under-25 boundary, start/end dates and part-year treatment, whether qualifying income is salary/wages only or also allowances/bonuses/share benefits/director fees/schedular payments, and whether net self-employment profit qualifies. The Act definition of employment income supplies a legal reference point, not the policy's complete eligibility rule.

Keep separate line items for income tax, ACC earner levy, student-loan repayment, KiwiSaver/other payroll deductions, and transfers/credits. For a tax-only scenario, hold non-tax rules fixed and state that assumption. For a broader disposable-income scenario, implement the applicable ACC, loan, benefit-abatement, and family-scheme-income rules explicitly and version them by their effective dates. IRD notes PAYE includes the earners' levy, so comparing PAYE withholding totals without separating components would incorrectly treat an unchanged levy as tax saved.

This is a static incidence calculation. It does not estimate changes to labour supply, hours, wages, employer costs, take-up, age eligibility behavior, consumption, savings, GDP, or financing. Those require separate behavioural/macro assumptions and empirical validation.

## Aggregate revenue evidence and recommendation

The tax value for supplied individual inputs can be calculated deterministically once the policy schema pins down the items above. Aggregate revenue requires weighting the individual tax change over the target population. The public sources found provide complementary but differently defined marginals:

- IRD has annual wage/salary amounts and counts by income band, but the published description does not show those bands crossed with age.
- IRD has taxable-income/tax totals by income band and some separate age-based extracts, but these are not a recurring complete age × qualifying employment income × other taxable income table. Taxable income is also not equivalent to employment income.
- Stats NZ publishes age-specific employment counts and survey income summaries; these can support population controls and plausibility checks but not, on the material verified here, an exact distribution of annual tax units jointly by age, annual qualifying earnings, other income, family situation, loan status, and benefit eligibility.
- HILS may help calibrate a grouped-data estimator, but public outputs are survey summaries, some cells are suppressed, samples have error, and 2024/25 estimates need the published quality notice checked. Its IDI calibration inputs are restricted administrative data, not a permission to use IDI microdata.

**Recommendation:** Do not report a point estimate for aggregate revenue from the currently verified public tables alone. First inspect the downloaded IRD and Stats NZ tables and document their actual dimensions. If they still lack the necessary joint age/income data, request aggregate cross-tabulations from IRD/Stats NZ through public information channels. If only grouped public data are available, produce clearly labelled scenario bounds or a survey-based estimate only after stating the within-band allocation, population weights, taxable-vs-qualifying income mapping, and assumptions for other income/credits; propagate sampling and band-allocation uncertainty. A range must not be described as an official forecast or exact costing. If suitable data are not released, the correct output is “aggregate revenue not estimable from available public aggregates,” with the missing cross-tabulation specified.

## Risks and open requirements

- The user has not yet selected which categories in the broad statutory employment-income definition are exempt or decided the self-employment treatment. The policy interface must ask rather than silently choose.
- The proposed example starts in April 2027. Tax rates, ACC, student-loan thresholds, and Working for Families rules must be read from law effective on each simulated date. Budget 2026 changes to Working for Families take effect 1 April 2027, so current guidance is not the final 2027 rule set.
- Aggregate annual taxpayer records can count people with mixed income, transfers, multiple jobs, non-resident status, or short employment periods. The exemption's calculation basis (per payment vs annual aggregate tax) must be settled in legislation and reflected in estimation.
- Public tax statistics are revised: IRD notes the latest 2026 workbook revised prior years. Preserve each downloaded source file and retrieval date; backtests and historical cost estimates should distinguish as-published information from latest-revised outcomes.
- Do not infer population shares from IRD taxpayer counts alone: IRD's under-21 response explicitly says it does not hold all age-group population denominators.
- Current static numbers cannot establish behavioural or causal policy effects. Any change in employment, hours, earnings, or GDP is outside this evidence and requires a separate identification strategy.
